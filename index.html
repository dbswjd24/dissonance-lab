Sensory dissonance as critical-band interaction

When two sinusoids are close in frequency they excite overlapping regions of the basilar membrane. Below about 15-20 Hz apart we hear beats. Further apart, the fluctuation is too fast to follow and is heard as roughness. Once the tones are more than about one critical bandwidth apart, they are resolved and the roughness disappears. Plomp and Levelt (1965) measured this curve and proposed that the dissonance of complex tones is the sum of the roughness between every pair of their partials.

Models implemented

  • Sethares (1993). A parametric fit to the Plomp-Levelt curve: d = min(a₁,a₂)·(e−3.51·s·Δf − e−5.75·s·Δf), with s = 0.24 / (0.0207·fmin + 18.96) so that the peak follows the critical band.
  • Hutchinson & Knopoff (1978) / Parncutt (1989). g(y) = (e·4y·e−4y)², where y is the frequency difference measured in critical bandwidths. The weights are a₁a₂ and the total is normalised by Σa². Two bandwidth scales are available:
    • Zwicker & Terhardt (1980): CB = 25 + 75(1 + 1.4(f/1000)²)0.69
    • Glasberg & Moore (1990) ERB, as in Unit 4: ERB = 24.7(4.37f/1000 + 1)

What the model predicts

  • Timbre matters. Pure tones show no dissonance past about a minor third. Harmonic tones produce dips at simple ratios (2/1, 3/2, 4/3, 5/4, 5/3, 6/5), because that is where their upper partials line up.
  • Tuning follows timbre (Sethares 2005). Stretch the partials and the "octave" dip moves with them. In this model the octave is consonant only because harmonic spectra make it so.
  • Register matters. Critical bands are wider in the bass, so the same interval is rougher when played low. This fits the orchestration rule of thumb about keeping thirds out of the low register.
  • Bottom-up only. The model leaves out top-down factors (Unit 2, Unit 4): enculturation, voice-leading context, stream segregation (Wright & Bregman), and Terhardt's distinction between sensory and musical consonance. Where it disagrees with listeners, those factors are the likely cause.

Testing it

The Python script python/analysis/validate.py correlates each model's predicted consonance with Malmberg's (1918) ranking of the 12 dyads and with the ratings you collect in the Listening experiment tab. It also compares them with a simple-ratio (Tenney height) baseline.
